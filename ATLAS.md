# Class Atlas operations

The Week 1 Atlas is a map-first studio exercise. Each student begins with a place they know in Japan and an engineering idea, object, infrastructure, or problem connected to it. It might have originated in Japan and traveled outward, or arrived from elsewhere and changed in Japan. They choose another nation to investigate and add a dated trace, evidence link, video, or question. Additional nations are branches of inquiry, not forced sequential travel. The filmstrip orders moments in the student's inquiry and makes elapsed years visible as gaps. Selecting a frame lights up its marker and place name; selecting a marker returns to its frame. Left/right controls and keyboard arrows move between frames. Each transition briefly draws a curved dotted geographic connection between the selected moments. This visualizes a change in narrative focus, **not** a claim about a real flight route or a necessary linear flow of technology.

The built-in Mina journey is explicitly fictional: Japan introduces urban rail as a system; Manila asks how a first subway is built and who bears its social cost; Delhi asks how an existing network expands; Jakarta asks how flood exposure affects underground design. JICA project, objection, and design-study records support the project details, while the student questions remain invented examples. The 2026 Jakarta date marks Mina's question, **not** the date of the cited 2013 study or a completed project. Students can choose a marker color and a one- or two-grapheme symbol, including Japanese characters or emoji; the marker code can display a vetted local photo asset later, after student consent and a photo-submission workflow are designed.

The second built-in example, `atlas.html#yoh`, is a draft of Yoh's own story, based on his account: his father's doctoral study in Japan; 1964 fieldwork at IRRI in the Philippines and meeting Yoh's mother; CIAT and Yoh's 1971 birth in Colombia; Bangkok in 1982 and regional work in Vietnam, Indonesia, and the Philippines; UCLA from 1995; Banda Aceh after the 2004 tsunami; and the 2011 Japan disasters, Fukushima, and *Human Error*. The three regional visits are labeled **1982+** to mean "during the Bangkok-based years," not assigned invented exact years or cities; country-level and regional phases use approximate map anchors. The Fukushima chapter is labeled **after 2011** rather than given an unverified filming year. This example is not a student submission and is not posted to the class intake. Four visual frames use existing course assets: a present-day IRRI image, a later cassava book cover, Yoh's Banda Aceh photo, and a still from *Human Error*. The IRRI and book images illustrate those chapters but do not depict the 1964 or 1971 events themselves. IRRI's Los Baños and CIAT's Palmira locations are linked to their current official pages in the corresponding frames; those pages verify locations, not the family's biographical timeline.

`atlas.html#studio` overlays the available journeys on one map and a shared time axis. It currently contains Yoh's draft, Mina's simulated example, and a student's locally saved draft if that browser has one; it does **not** yet show all students across devices. Each project occupies its own timeline lane, and selecting a point opens that chapter.

The page stores a student's journey in that browser's local storage and sends the complete journey record to an invisible Google Form each time they add a hop. The form has fields `Name or alias`, `Atlas title`, and `Atlas record` (JSON). Its POST is `no-cors`: the client cannot verify Google accepted it. The public map feed is **not connected**, so sent student stories do not yet appear on other devices or on the public map. The interface says this plainly. Students should use the same browser when extending a journey until a reliable shared read/write path is active.

The form does not collect email addresses. Yoh approved immediate publication of the raw response tab, including timestamps, but that tab still needs to be published and anonymously verified. In the linked response Sheet, use **File → Share → Publish to web**, select **フォームの回答 1**, choose **CSV**, and publish. Paste that public CSV URL into `feedUrl` in `assets/atlas-config.js`; implement and test the read client before turning on shared display. Confirm the published CSV headers, submit a course-authorized test journey, and verify it appears from a signed-out browser. A `no-cors` request resolving is never proof of acceptance.

Never solicit private addresses, student contact details, or a portrait without consent. Student-provided text must remain text, not HTML; external links must be restricted to HTTP(S). Keep simulation clearly labelled, distinct from student submissions.
