# Class Atlas operations

The Week 1 Atlas opens on the shared studio map and timeline. “Add my journey” asks for a class password and email, then begins a two-step author activity. Step 1 records an alias, marker color and symbol, and a starting place in Japan; an explicit option permits starting anywhere. Step 2 asks the student to choose a connected country and record an engineering idea, tags, year, optional link, image, and evidence or open question. The idea may have originated in Japan and traveled outward, or arrived from elsewhere and changed in Japan. Additional nations are branches of inquiry, not forced sequential travel. The filmstrip orders moments in the student's inquiry and makes elapsed years visible as gaps. Selecting a frame lights up its marker and place name; selecting a marker returns to its frame. Left/right controls and keyboard arrows move between frames. Each transition briefly draws a curved dotted geographic connection between the selected moments. This visualizes a change in narrative focus, **not** a claim about a real flight route or a necessary linear flow of technology.

The built-in Mina journey is explicitly fictional: Japan introduces urban rail as a system; Manila asks how a first subway is built and who bears its social cost; Delhi asks how an existing network expands; Jakarta asks how flood exposure affects underground design. JICA project, objection, and design-study records support the project details, while the student questions remain invented examples. The 2026 Jakarta date marks Mina's question, **not** the date of the cited 2013 study or a completed project. Students can choose a marker color and a one- or two-grapheme symbol, including Japanese characters or emoji; the marker code can display a vetted local photo asset later, after student consent and a photo-submission workflow are designed.

The second built-in example, `atlas.html#yoh`, is a draft of Yoh's own story, based on his account: his father's doctoral study in Japan; 1964 fieldwork at IRRI in the Philippines and meeting Yoh's mother; CIAT and Yoh's 1971 birth in Colombia; Bangkok in 1982 and regional work in Vietnam, Indonesia, and the Philippines; UCLA from 1995; Banda Aceh after the 2004 tsunami; and the 2011 Japan disasters, Fukushima, and *Human Error*. The three regional visits are labeled **1982+** to mean "during the Bangkok-based years," not assigned invented exact years or cities; country-level and regional phases use approximate map anchors. The Fukushima chapter is labeled **after 2011** rather than given an unverified filming year. This example is not a student submission and is not posted to the class intake. Four visual frames use existing course assets: a present-day IRRI image, a later cassava book cover, Yoh's Banda Aceh photo, and a still from *Human Error*. The IRRI and book images illustrate those chapters but do not depict the 1964 or 1971 events themselves. IRRI's Los Baños and CIAT's Palmira locations are linked to their current official pages in the corresponding frames; those pages verify locations, not the family's biographical timeline.

`atlas.html` opens the available journeys on one map and a shared time axis. It contains Yoh's draft, Mina's simulated example, and published class stories. Each project occupies its own timeline lane, and selecting a point opens that chapter.

The class password is kept only in Cloudflare's `CLASS_PASSWORD` runtime secret. Signing in saves the normalized email privately in D1 and gives the browser a temporary token; neither email nor token appears on the public map. Only journeys recorded under the signed-in email expose edit/delete actions next to the active marker. Revision checks prevent silently overwriting a newer edit; reload and review the latest version if a conflict occurs. The email is **not verified**: anyone who knows the shared class password could enter another student's email, so this is a trust-based class workflow, not strong account security.

Images are compressed in the browser and stored as D1 BLOBs under 700 KB each. Their public image URLs are added to the journey frame only after an authorized upload. Cloudflare's documented per-BLOB limit is 2 MB. The Google Form settings remain in `assets/atlas-config.js` as a rollback option, but the Worker is the active route. Its source, deployment configuration, and schema live in `atlas-backend/` and deploy from GitHub. An authenticated production create/edit/upload/delete still needs checking with an authorized class account.

Do not ask students to include private details in public story text or an image without consent. Student-provided text remains text, not HTML; external links are restricted to HTTP(S). Keep simulations clearly labelled, distinct from student submissions.
